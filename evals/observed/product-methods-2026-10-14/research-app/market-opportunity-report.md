# Hypothetical opportunity assessment

The fixture permits an illustrative annual list-price calculation, not a finding that a real opportunity exists. Geography is a synthetic region; source is hypothetical fixture assumptions with no empirical source date. Product, customer problem, budget owner, user/account/location relationship, buying frequency and real geography are unspecified. For this exercise only, the decision is whether the numeric inputs justify validation work; a falsifiable demand hypothesis would require eligible distinct buyers to have a problem worth paying to solve, which is currently untested.

| Input | Definition/source | Status and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo: 3,000 buying units | Fixture segment, $1,000 USD per year | Assumed count and list price; no observed buyers, transactions, tax or discount terms. |
| Agency: 1,000 buying units | Fixture segment, $100 USD per month = $1,200 per year | Assumed count and list price; compatible annualization, no evidence of actual payment. |
| Overlap: 500 buying units | Fixture says included in both sets; do not bill twice | Known hypothetical overlap count; assignment to one annual price not supplied. |
| Reach, demand, willingness to pay | Not supplied/not measured | Cannot infer addressable distribution, adoption, obtainable market, ARR, cash flow or margin. |

Distinct buying units = 3,000 + 1,000 − 500 = **3,500**. Solo-only = 2,500; agency-only = 500. Assuming every distinct unit buys exactly one of the supplied segment offers for one full year, non-overlap list-price value = 2,500 × $1,000 + 500 × $1,200 = $3,100,000/year. The overlap contributes between 500 × $1,000 and 500 × $1,200 depending on assignment. The resulting conditional full-participation value is **$3,600,000–$3,700,000 USD/year**.

Let `x` be overlap units assigned agency pricing, `0 ≤ x ≤ 500`; annual value is `3,600,000 + 200*x`. Each assignment from solo to agency changes the illustration by $200/year. At x=0, 250 and 500 the values are $3.60m, $3.65m and $3.70m. This is uncertainty from the actual missing assignment, not an arbitrary optimistic adoption scenario. The naive sum, 3,000 × $1,000 + 1,000 × $100 × 12 = $4,200,000/year, bills overlap twice and overstates this single-offer illustration by $500,000–$600,000. The midpoint is merely an assignment example, not a forecast.

The bound assumes the offered price for an overlapping unit is one of these two prices; it is not a bound on real market revenue. Some units might buy neither, have different eligibility, receive negotiated discounts or be defined incompatibly. The fixture cannot tell whether solo/agency buying units correspond to real independent accounts. There is no validated reach/adoption rate; multiplying by an arbitrary market-share percentage would not establish obtainable revenue. If future validation supplies eligible reachable buyers and observed price acceptance, rebuild the model at the deduplicated buying-unit level. Costs, capacity, churn, collection, tax, discounts and switching effort are absent, so profit or operational feasibility cannot be calculated.

No customer research, contradictory interviews, switching evidence, distribution evidence or willingness-to-pay measurement is supplied. Manual workflows, doing nothing and incumbent solutions are substitute hypotheses to investigate, not observed substitutes for an unspecified product. Competitor list prices, if later gathered, would remain price context rather than proof of willingness to pay.

Supported decision: retain this as an arithmetic exercise and define the problem, buyer and overlap assignment before relying on it commercially. The largest current uncertainty is whether reachable buyers have paid demand at all; it dominates the $100,000 price-assignment spread. A later authorized validation should test the problem and existing adequate substitutes, buyer/budget authority, actual access to distinct buying units and credible price acceptance. A finding of adequate existing workflows, costly switching or no budget would weaken the opportunity; credible observed buying behavior and a reconciled reachable list would strengthen it. No outreach, ad spend, purchase, provider access, live experiment or current-market retrieval occurred.

`reproduce.py` executes the arithmetic using the copied original hypothetical input and asserts the deduplicated counts and endpoints. `checks.json` stores numeric outputs. Those successful local calculations establish arithmetic only; they are not empirical market verification or a product feasibility result.
